# Persona Positioning

This document helps admissions agents and AI assistants position the Forge Writing Retreat appropriately based on the applicant's background, motivations, and concerns.

---

# Persona 1 — Aspiring Writer ("I've Always Wanted to Write")

## Who They Are

People who do not fully identify themselves as writers yet but feel strongly drawn toward storytelling.

They often say things like:

- "I've had this idea for years."
- "I don't know where to start."
- "I'm scared I'm not good enough."

---

## Positioning Strategy

Position Forge Writing Retreat as a place for people who know there is a story inside them but need the right environment, structure, and encouragement to finally begin writing seriously.

Focus on:

- Safety
- Seriousness
- Mentorship
- Creative courage
- Beginning the journey

Frame the retreat as the turning point from **"Someday"** to **"I finally started."**

---

## What to Emphasize

- You do not need to be famous or published.
- You do not need a perfect draft.
- The environment helps people reconnect with their voice.
- The retreat gives dedicated time and seriousness that everyday life rarely provides.

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not imply that only experienced writers belong here.
- Do not make beginners feel underqualified.
- Do not overemphasize credentials or previous achievements.

---

## Best Closing Line

> "If you've been carrying a story inside you for a long time, this retreat is designed to help you finally begin writing it with confidence."

---

# Persona 2 — Serious Writer / Work-in-Progress Storyteller

## Who They Are

People who are already writing, have a partial manuscript or screenplay, or take writing seriously.

Typical concerns include:

- Feedback quality
- Mentor expertise
- Writing time
- Meaningful progress
- Creative outcomes

---

## Positioning Strategy

Present Forge Writing Retreat as a high-intent immersive environment for writers who want genuine movement on an existing project.

Focus on:

- Creative rigor
- Guided mentorship
- Focused writing time
- Constructive feedback
- Serious writing community

Position it as a place to sharpen the work, gain clarity, and overcome creative blocks.

---

## What to Emphasize

- Serious environment rather than casual hobby learning.
- Mentorship and feedback are central.
- The retreat respects writing as both craft and process.
- Progress and momentum matter more than passive inspiration.

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not oversell inspiration alone.
- Do not make it sound like a recreational getaway.
- Do not imply guaranteed publishing or commercial success.

---

## Best Closing Line

> "If you've already started writing but feel stuck or want deeper guidance, Forge provides the environment and mentorship to help move your work forward."

---

# Persona 3 — Working Professional / Creative Reset Seeker

## Who They Are

People balancing careers, routines, responsibilities, or burnout who say things like:

- "I never get time."
- "I've been postponing my writing."
- "I need to get away and focus."

---

## Positioning Strategy

Present Forge Writing Retreat as an intentional creative reset.

Frame it as an opportunity to step away from everyday distractions and immerse oneself in a protected environment dedicated entirely to writing.

Highlight the value of changing both environment and mindset.

---

## What to Emphasize

- This experience is difficult to recreate at home.
- The retreat creates emotional and mental space.
- Community reduces isolation.
- It is an investment in clarity, creative voice, and direction.

---

## What to Avoid

- Do not describe it as a vacation.
- Do not minimize the commitment required.
- Do not suggest it is simply a break from work.

---

## Best Closing Line

> "Sometimes the biggest breakthrough comes from giving yourself uninterrupted time and the right environment to finally focus on the story that matters most."
